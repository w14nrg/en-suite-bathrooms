What changes the final price?

The hidden work matters as much as the visible finish

The product list is only part of a renovation. The existing property and the route needed to complete the work properly can have an equal or greater effect on the final cost.

Layout changes

Moving toilets, showers, baths or basins can require new drainage routes, floor work, boxing and additional plumbing.

Existing condition

Rotten floors, damaged walls, leaking pipework and previous poor workmanship may only become clear after strip-out.

Specification

Tiles, sanitaryware, brassware, shower valves, screens, vanity units and bespoke products vary enormously in price.

Access and logistics

Upper-floor flats, restricted parking, long carrying routes and difficult waste removal can affect labour and delivery planning.

Electrics and ventilation

New circuits, lighting, mirrors, extractor fans and electrical upgrades may require appropriately qualified specialists.

Building work

New walls, doorways, ceilings, floor strengthening, structural work and extensive making good increase the scope.

Price questions

Before requesting a quotation

Does the agreed price include everything?

Yes, once the survey, budget, specification and scope are agreed, the written quotation is for the agreed finished job. It sets out the labour, products, materials, preparation, installation, finishing and project coordination included in that price. Anything excluded, optional or dependent on hidden defects is made clear before work is booked.

Why do some bathrooms start from £4,000–£5,000 while others start from £8,000?

A basic rental, HMO or simple like-for-like bathroom can sometimes be kept cheaper by using practical products, keeping the layout the same and avoiding major hidden repair work. A proper homeowner bathroom refurbishment normally has a higher finish level, more preparation, better products and more time spent on the finished detail, so it usually starts from around £8,000.

Are the prices on this page fixed?

No. They are broad guidance to help you judge the likely level of investment. Your final quotation will be based on the property, agreed design, specification and complete scope of work. Once that scope is agreed, the quotation confirms the price for the agreed package.

Do the figures include products?

Yes. The guide figures are complete agreed-project budget guidance, not labour-only figures. The final quotation will show the agreed products, tiles, materials, labour, fitting, specified tiling and specified finishing included. Premium fittings, specialist finishes or unusually expensive tiles can move the total above the guide.

Can you quote from photographs?

Photographs can help with an initial conversation, but they do not show drainage routes, floor condition, pressure, ventilation, access or the work hidden behind existing finishes. A proper quotation may require a site visit and measured survey.

Why does the quotation take 5–7 working days?

The time is used to review the survey, develop the direction, check products and practical requirements, obtain contractor and material costs, calculate the project properly and assemble a clear quotation pack.
