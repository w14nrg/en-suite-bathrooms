PROJECT · SHOWER-ROOM RENOVATION

Lille Road, Fulham

A dated quadrant shower room taken back to the walls and floor and rebuilt as a warm, modern shower room. Once the old tiles came off, the stud wall behind them needed replacing.

WhatsApp Nicholas

Before, during and after

BeforeStrip-outAfter

What we did

  • Old enclosure, tiles and fittings stripped out, and the floor opened to rework the waste and supply pipework.
  • Failed studwork found behind the old tiles replaced with new studs, then boarded and waterproofed before tiling.
  • A new return wall built to form the shower area, with a slimline tray, brushed-brass framed sliding door and thermostatic rainfall shower.
  • Large-format porcelain to the floor and half-height walls, with a fluted wood vanity, new WC and brass towel rail.

What the client wanted

The original room had a curved corner enclosure that took up a lot of the floor and made the shower itself feel cramped. The brief was a full-length shower, a brighter room and a warmer brass finish, without the room feeling any smaller.

What we found after the strip-out

Once the old tiles, enclosure and suite were removed, sections of the walls and studwork around the old shower were in poor condition, with staining behind the old valve. This could not simply be tiled over. We replaced the failing studwork and formed a new side wall with a return to take the new shower tray and enclosure.

How it was rebuilt

  • Full strip-out of tiles, enclosure, suite and floor finish
  • Failing studwork replaced and a new side wall with a return formed
  • Walls lined with NoMorePly cement boards; floor tiles laid over a decoupling membrane
  • Luxe81 Delta White 595 × 595mm porcelain to walls and floor
  • Merlyn MBOX 1000mm brushed-brass enclosure and Crosswater Central brushed-brass shower
  • Fluted wall-hung vanity, brass tap and brass towel radiator
  • Walls finished in Farrow & Ball paint
