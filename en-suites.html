What a new en-suite involves

The shower, toilet and basin are the visible part. Behind them can be new walls, a door, soil and waste pipework, hot and cold water, ventilation, electrics, waterproofing, heating, tiling and making good the bedroom.

That is why our first question is not “which suite do you like?” It is whether the room can support a proper en-suite without leaving you with awkward access, unreliable drainage or an unusable bedroom.

New partitions, door opening and room formation where requiredSoil, waste and water-service planning before fittings are chosenVentilation, electrics, waterproofing and heating considered as part of the roomSanitaryware, shower, tiling and final finishing coordinated as one project

What we need to know

A compact room can be perfectly workable, but only when the services and remaining bedroom are considered at the same time.

01

Usable space

We measure the bedroom, door positions, windows, wardrobes and circulation so the new room does not destroy the space it is meant to improve.

02

Soil & waste route

The WC needs a sensible discharge route and the shower and basin need reliable falls. The route can influence the whole position of the en-suite.

03

Water supply

We look at hot-water provision, pressure and flow so the proposed shower and fittings suit the property rather than only the brochure.

04

Ventilation

A new internal shower room needs a proper ventilation strategy. We consider the route and practical installation before the room is enclosed.

05

Structure & access

Floor construction, ceiling height, access for pipework and the route for materials all affect how straightforward the build will be.

The installation

The exact sequence varies, but a properly formed en-suite normally moves through these stages rather than jumping straight to fitting products.

1

Survey & layout

Measure the space, check the property and agree where the shower, WC, basin and door can realistically sit.

2

Form the room

Create or alter partitions and openings where needed, while planning access for services and future maintenance.

3

First fix

Install or alter soil, waste and water pipework together with the required electrical and ventilation routes.

4

Prepare & waterproof

Prepare floors and walls, form shower areas and waterproof the wet zones before decorative finishes are installed.

5

Tile & fit out

Install tiles or agreed finishes, sanitaryware, brassware, shower enclosure, heating, lighting and accessories.

6

Test & hand over

Test the room, complete finishing details and leave the new en-suite ready for normal use.

New en-suite FAQs

Can you add an en-suite to an existing bedroom?

Often, yes. The deciding factors are usable bedroom space and whether we can create practical routes for the WC soil pipe, shower and basin waste, water supplies and ventilation. We check those before committing to a layout.

What is actually included in building a new en-suite?

Depending on the property, the work can include partitions, a new door, drainage, hot and cold water, ventilation, electrics, waterproofing, wall and floor finishes, sanitaryware, shower fittings, heating and making good the surrounding bedroom.

Can you build an en-suite where there is no nearby soil pipe?

Sometimes, but the drainage strategy becomes a key part of the survey. A conventional gravity route is preferable where it is practical; other solutions may be possible in suitable situations. We will not promise a layout until the route has been assessed.

How small can an en-suite be?

There is no useful single minimum that guarantees a good room. Shower size, door position, WC projection, basin depth, maintenance access and the remaining bedroom all matter. Compact rooms work best when every fitting is selected around the measured space.

How much does a new en-suite cost?

Many complete new en-suite projects fall around £12,000–£18,000+, although simpler layouts with nearby drainage and existing services can come in lower. The final quotation depends on the actual room, drainage route, building work and finish.

How long does it take?

Roughly two working weeks can be a sensible starting allowance for a straightforward project. Complex drainage, structural work, specialist finishes and property access can add time.

Do I have to buy products from you?

No. You can show us products you like and we can check them against the room and installation. The quotation will make clear what is supplied, what is an allowance and what is customer supplied.
