Cloakrooms and downstairs toilets

A small room that can make a big difference

A cloakroom or downstairs toilet is one of the most practical additions you can make to a home. It can reduce pressure on the main bathroom, make the property easier for guests and family, and add everyday convenience where the only bathroom is upstairs.

It can also improve how a property feels to future buyers or tenants. UK property guidance often suggests an extra WC or bathroom can add around 3–5% to a home’s value, with several sources quoting around 5% for a downstairs toilet. The real uplift depends on the property, location, layout and quality of finish.

The room may be compact, but the planning still matters. Drainage, water supply, ventilation, door position, basin size, boxing, tiling, lighting and flooring all need to be thought through properly before work starts.

Convenience, value and proper planning

A downstairs toilet needs the same practical thinking as a bathroom: drainage, ventilation, fittings, finishes and a layout that works in the real property.

Why buyers and families notice it

A downstairs WC is not only about resale. It changes how a home works day to day. Guests do not need to go upstairs, children and older relatives have easier access, and the main bathroom is under less pressure during busy mornings.

The strongest results come when the cloakroom feels like a considered part of the home rather than a squeezed-in afterthought. Layout, ventilation, lighting, basin choice, door position and finishes all matter.
