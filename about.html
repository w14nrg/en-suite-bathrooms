About En-Suites & Bathrooms

Builder-led bathroom design from a Fulham trade background

Born and raised in Fulham. Trained in real West London houses.

En-Suites & Bathrooms was founded by Nicholas Griffith, who grew up around the building trade and started working in his father’s building business at 16. The business brings together hands-on building experience, bathroom installation knowledge and modern design presentation for customers planning bathrooms and new en-suites.

Local roots

A lifetime around building, plumbing and bathrooms

Nicholas Griffith was born and raised in Fulham and has worked in the building trade since the age of 16. Long before En-Suites & Bathrooms existed, he was working in real Fulham houses with his father’s building company, learning the trade on site rather than from a desk.

That background matters. Older West London properties often come with awkward pipe routes, uneven floors, tired bathrooms, hidden services, poor ventilation and rooms that need practical thinking before the design is finalised.

En-Suites & Bathrooms is built from that experience: design that looks good, but only after the room, the plumbing and the build have been properly understood.

Why it matters

The design is only right if it can be built properly

A bathroom can look perfect on a screen but fail on site if the drainage is wrong, the water pressure is poor, the floor levels do not work, the ventilation is missed or the chosen products do not suit the room.

This is why En-Suites & Bathrooms takes a builder-led approach. The design is shaped around the look you want, but also around the practical installation route that makes the room work in the real property.

Drainage

Soil pipe positions, waste falls, pipe routes and access points need thinking through early.

Water pressure

Showers and valves need to suit the property’s hot and cold water supply.

Preparation

Walls, floors, waterproofing, tiling and fixing strength affect how the room lasts.

Real homes

Older Fulham and West London homes often need practical thinking before design decisions are made.
